## Subject association - interest organisation

The most important task of a subject association is to act as an interest organisation for students and to make sure that the students' voice is heard in the university's decision-making. In departments with a student-friendly administrative culture (like ours), the subject association often takes a stand on matters under preparation. Active members of the association discuss, among other things, various topics related to studies and the position of students with the department's staff. Above all, the subject association is an independent influencer that also takes initiatives when needed, based on feedback received from students.

### Student representatives in administration

Most of the influencing happens through the student representatives in the administration. The representatives are nominally independent of the subject associations and represent all students. They are chosen for their positions on the proposal of the faculty's nomination committee, which is approved by the board of the Student Union. There are many decision-making bodies, and the university's administrative practice is based on setting up a working group, committee or board to prepare large matters, usually including one or more student representatives.

Bodies with decision-making power include the management groups at department level, the faculty councils at faculty level and the university board led by the rector at university level. Student representatives were granted to all of them in the early 1990s. A representative is expected to have a view on student advocacy, possible earlier experience and connections to organisations operating within the university. Most important, however, is being ready to take one's own initiative, because a significant part of the real power lies with various preparatory bodies; matters are usually so broad and complex that the decision-making bodies do not have time to familiarise themselves with all of them sufficiently. It is therefore important that students' views come up already at the preparation stage, when matters can be noticed in time and proposals can be influenced more easily.

## Want to join?

Parties and shared pastimes also have their place in student advocacy. Without them it would be impossible for a subject association to get people involved in advocacy work. In TKO-äly advocacy work is above all the task of the board and the active members, especially the chair, the vice chair and the study officers. Other people are always needed too, as there is plenty of work for those willing to do it, and the association would not otherwise get anywhere in its activities. TKO-äly announces new tasks, for example, on Telegram channels and the mailing list. It is therefore good to follow them, but taking your own initiative is the most important thing.
