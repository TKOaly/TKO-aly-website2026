**A short summary of what sitsit, the academic table party, involves.**

Sitsit are table parties where you usually enjoy a three-course meal (starter, main course and dessert) with drinks, and of course singing. Sitsit are held in almost all larger student organisations at different universities.

The food is usually prepared and served by a sitsit team put together from the ranks of the organising student organisation, so the price (drinks included) is quite reasonable, usually between fifteen and twenty euros. The drinks sometimes include a welcome drink (depending on the price), usually a few pours of wine with the meal, a snaps, and punch or a similar drink with the coffee.

Compared with annual celebrations (vuosijuhlat), sitsit are a less formal occasion, and "smart casual" is enough for clothing unless there is another theme. A good point of comparison is what you would wear to a relative's 50th birthday party: do not turn up in sweatpants and a T-shirt, but you do not need to dig a suit out of the closet either.

The seats at the tables are usually named in advance (seating plan) so that different year classes get mixed and everyone does not end up next to only their friends. Most often sitsit have a song leader who starts the songs so that each table does not make noise at its own pace. Of course anyone can start a song at a suitable moment, but it is considerate to tell the others what is being sung and on which page of the song sheet (läsy) or songbook the words can be found. The tunes of the songs are often from familiar songs, and you pick up the stranger ones quickly by ear. You also do not need to be a golden-voiced singer to dare to open your mouth at a student party. Usually a song booklet with the words is handed out at the seats.

As for table manners, according to normal etiquette you must not start cold dishes until the whole table has received their portions; with warm dishes you can start when those sitting nearby have received their food. During a song it is not appropriate to clink cutlery (eat) or make any other noise apart from singing. There is no need to worry about the food cooling down; at a proper party there is so much singing that you will not have time to finish the main course warm without wolfing it down.

### A few keywords:

- _avec_ the person you sit next to at the party. Everyone should be considerate towards their avec and consider various traditional gestures, for example lifting the chair to the table when arriving and leaving, pouring water, etc.
- _plaseeraus_ (seating plan) usually the seats at the tables are named in advance.
- _skoolaus_ (toasting) an essential part of sitsit are the drinking songs, which end with a toast. The official pattern is not always followed in all organisations, but this is how it goes: usually you first toast diagonally to the right, then diagonally to the left and then to the person sitting opposite.
