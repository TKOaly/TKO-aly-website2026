## TKO-äly's ribbon

TKO-äly's ribbon is black-yellow-black in colour. It is worn as an insignia at all suitable festive occasions, such as the annual celebrations and other table parties of the association and of other organisations and nations; that is, whenever the invitation mentions academic decorations or ribbons. TKO-äly members can redeem a TKO-äly ribbon from the members of TKO-äly's board in Gurula.

## TKO-äly's Rubber Duck pin (Kumiankka)

The pin is worn together with the TKO-äly ribbon or the ribbon of honour at the same occasions. The Rubber Duck can be awarded to members of the association who have shown responsibility and initiative in their position, done longer-term work in smaller positions or otherwise worked for the benefit of the association. Signs of this can be a clearly well-handled responsible position in a team, several years of activity as an official, or significant participation in a one-off project. The basis can also be a combination of the above or some other equivalent achievement. The badge of merit can also be awarded to a person outside the association or a stakeholder. The Rubber Duck pin also serves as a board member's badge of representation from the start of the term, after which the board member may keep the pin in recognition of their board work.

The Rubber Duck is awarded by the board itself at its own meeting. Members of the association can propose recipients of the badge of merit to the board. 7-10 Rubber Duck pins are awarded per year.

## TKO-äly's Knowledge Duck pin (Tietoankka)

The Knowledge Duck can be awarded to members of the association who have shown exceptionally great interest in the association's activities over a longer period or have otherwise significantly promoted the association's activities. Signs of this can be, for example, several years of active work in a position that requires responsibility and initiative, or leading responsibility in significant one-off projects that benefit the subject association and develop its activities. The basis can also be a combination of both of the above or some other equivalent achievement. The badge of merit can also be awarded to a person outside the association or a stakeholder.

The Knowledge Duck is awarded by a working group that includes a representative of the board and interested persons who have received the Knowledge Duck or the ribbon of honour. The working group makes a proposal, whose approval is decided by the board. Members of the working group cannot propose themselves as recipients of the badge of merit. 2-5 Knowledge Duck pins are awarded per year.

## Ribbon of honour

Since 1996, former chairs and the most significant active members of the association have been rewarded at the annual celebrations with TKO-äly's yellow ribbon of honour. The ribbon tradition was started by Minna Romppainen, and the list of recipients was compiled by Timo Oksala in 2006.

TKO-äly's yellow ribbon of honour can be received by a person who has been active in the association for at least three years, of which at least one year as chair, vice chair or in another demanding and important position.

The person must have shown activity and a sense of responsibility in carrying out their duties and have conveyed a positive image of the association to the outside. Through their own actions the person has promoted the development of the association's activities and has otherwise set an example for the association's other members.

The decision to award the ribbon of honour is usually made by the chair of the board together with the persons who have previously received the ribbon, but other opinions may also be heard before the actual decision is made.

### On wearing the TKO-äly ribbon and pins

![](/attachments/files/8/ribbon.png)

The colours of the association are worn in a manner befitting their value, men on the right shoulder and women on the left shoulder. A good mnemonic is that academic ribbon traditions trace their roots to the sword's sash. A right-handed man carries the sword on his left side, so the ribbon runs over the right shoulder. Women can also make a rosette of their ribbon, as the ribbon must not touch bare skin. Women wear the rosette on the left side, like the ribbon. A suitable place is an area about the size of a palm below the shoulder. If a woman wants to wear the ribbon long with a strapless evening gown, it should be arranged to run from the top edge of the dress, not over the shoulder.

If you own the colours of several organisations or nations, it is stylish to wear at most three ribbons at a time. Of course, the rosette or ribbon of your own association is on top. Some active people accumulate considerably more ribbons than the three that can be worn at once. Unfortunately there is no way to wear all the important ribbons at once. For men, attaching the other ribbons to a piece of cardboard and carrying it in the breast pocket is not a recommended way. Also not recommended is the ribbon sometimes seen on women hanging from the waist, to which other ribbons are attached as bows, as ribbons are definitely not meant to be worn around the knees.

Ribbons and pins are not worn with military uniforms or other official uniforms, or with national costumes. Overalls, sportswear and other informal outfits are also unsuitable. You sometimes see some insignia worn with jeans and sweaters, but a good basic rule is a neat outfit; the insignia do not belong with more casual wear.

Badge-of-merit pins are worn either at the same time as the ribbon or separately. The pin can be worn on the left lapel of the jacket and in other outfits at the corresponding place.

Badge-of-merit pins and ribbons of honour have traditionally been handed out at the association's annual celebrations.
